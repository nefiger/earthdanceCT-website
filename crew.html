Crew Makes the Gathering

The hands that hold the space

Long before the gates open, and long after the last set ends, Earthdance runs on people doing the unglamorous, essential work — kitchens, gates, parking, and the thousand small things a gathering needs to hold together. Meet some of the crew bringing 2026 to life.

Photo: Jaco Brewer Photography
Meet the Crew

Earthdance 2026 crew

Crew

Bryony Leach

Bryony notices details and keeps track of objects — handy on a build. She brings filmset construction experience and first aid, helped set up the BEAT and HUMANITY warm-ups, and has been a fire performer at the last several Earthdances.

Instagram

Conrad Swart

Conrad's been to Earthdance every year, often in different provinces, and it usually lands on his birthday weekend. He does demarcation and signage for AfrikaBurn, and this year he's swapping the dancefloor for the build crew for the first time.

Jarryt Sean Layne

Jarryt's spent six years in film and events, focused on unit and site ops, with decor, stage builds and crew welfare behind him. He loves a hard day's work and good tunes, and he's bringing his PDP licence to help the build crew however it's needed.

Instagram

Jason St.Blaize Searle

Jason's a veteran builder, artisan and structural fabricator who has spent decades shaping South Africa's festival and counterculture landscape — most notably driving the massive kinetic wooden builds and performance effigies for AfrikaBurn. He's on the senior build crew this year, bringing the collective grit it takes to raise impossible structures in raw places.

Josef Klue

Josef's a rigger by trade and by handle, happy to roll wherever he's needed. He's on the build crew this year, helping build and rig the site at Kromrivier.

Instagram

Kei St.Blaize Searle

Kei's a designer and fabricator who practically grew up on festival build sites, surrounded by South Africa's large-scale structural art communities. He's rigging and carpentering on the build crew this year, and away from the site he turns his creative eye to documenting Cape Town's underground subcultures.

Instagram

Matthew Morgan Snyders

Matthew's a 25-year-old motorcycle mechanic who's been going to Vortex parties since 2018. It's his first party build, but he brings carpentry, electrical and decking experience, a head for heights and a good hand with power tools.

Instagram

Nico Storm

Nico's a creator, builder and people connector with 20+ years across hospitality leadership, event production, set design and experiential facilitation. He's on the build crew this year, turning ideas into moments people remember.

Niku Pendlebury

Niku grew up going to Earthdance and sees it as the place where the family comes together. He's on the build crew this year, holding space for the dreamers and helping make the site feel like home — and he's playing as Space Monk too.

Leading the Return

The people steering it home

Behind the crew, a small team carries Earthdance Cape Town year-round — the people who brought the festival back in 2025 and are doing it again for 2026.

Festival Custodian & Lead

Michael "Birdman" Aldridge

Birdman has spent decades at the meeting point of trance, consciousness and collective transformation, from Johannesburg's early techno raves through years of ceremony at Altered States to Earthdance's 1999 gathering in Dharamsala. When the previous organisers stepped back, he secured the licence from Earthdance Global and called the community together to bring the festival home in 2025, and continues as its custodian and lead for 2026.

Music & Programme

Mikey Dredd

Mikey came up in South Africa's late-90s and early-2000s psytrance scene, remembered for the marathon sets that helped define those early outdoor gatherings. He joined Earthdance production in 2008 and has worked across stage, sound and operations ever since — this year he's shaping the 2026 programme and co-hosting Frequency Check alongside Birdman.

Production Manager

Jennifer Walker

Jennifer is Earthdance Cape Town's production manager for 2026, an event producer and systems thinker with 25 years spent between complex systems and the people who use them. Her focus is making the festival sustainable without losing what makes it matter — building the conditions for people to arrive as co-creators, not just an audience.
